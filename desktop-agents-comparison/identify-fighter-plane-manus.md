### About the F-16 Fighting Falcon

The **F-16 Fighting Falcon** is a single-engine, supersonic, multirole tactical fighter aircraft developed by General Dynamics (now Lockheed Martin) for the United States Air Force. First flown in **1974**, it has become one of the most widely produced and operated jet fighters in history, with over **4,600 units** built and service in more than **25 countries**.

It is powered by a single **Pratt & Whitney F100** or **General Electric F110** turbofan engine, capable of speeds exceeding **Mach 2**. The aircraft is renowned for its agility, fly-by-wire flight control system, and the distinctive side-mounted air intake beneath the fuselage.

The two aircraft appear to be flying in a **tactical formation**, suggesting a training flight or patrol mission. The paint scheme appears to be a standard **air superiority grey** camouflage, common across many F-16 operators worldwide.

### Key Details of the Current Deployment

The Portuguese detachment, which took over from Italy on **March 31, 2026**, consists of **four F-16M aircraft** and approximately **95 personnel** (pilots, maintainers, and support staff). This marks the **ninth time** the Portuguese Air Force has participated in the NATO Air Policing mission, and the second time they have operated specifically from Ämari (previous rotations were from Šiauliai Air Base in Lithuania).

The aircraft are maintained on **Quick Reaction Alert (QRA)** around the clock, ready to identify, monitor, and if necessary intercept any air threat approaching Allied airspace over the Baltic region. The mission operates under the command and control of the **Combined Air Operations Centre (CAOC) Uedem**, Germany.
