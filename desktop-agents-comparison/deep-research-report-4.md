# macOS Agentic Tools for Office Work

## Scope and decision frame

This comparison is **not** about agentic software development. It evaluates **Manus 1.6**, **Claude Desktop** across its **Chat, Cowork, and Code** surfaces, and **Codex** as tools for a regular office worker on macOS: drafting documents, working with spreadsheets and slides, analysing CSVs and web/custom sources, organising local folders, producing interactive reports, turning meeting recordings into notes and memos, and connecting to business systems such as Jira, Confluence, Slack, and Microsoft 365 or SharePoint. The additional constraint is critical: an **EU-based company under GDPR** that prefers files to stay **local, in company systems, or in Microsoft 365/SharePoint**, and does **not** want staff casually exposing company files to the AI vendor. citeturn7view0turn7view1turn5view0turn21view7turn28view1

On that frame, the single most important question is **where the work actually happens**. “Agentic” tools differ less by model quality than by execution boundary: some patterns are heavily cloud-centred, some are genuinely local-first, and some mix both. The uploaded Claude Desktop architecture notes are especially valuable here: they show **Claude Chat** rendering Claude’s web app and talking to Anthropic infrastructure, while **Claude Cowork** runs a **local Linux VM on macOS** using Apple’s Virtualization framework and mounts host folders into that VM. The same material also shows Cowork’s network path being constrained through allowlisted domains, which is materially different from a pure browser-chat model. fileciteturn0file8 fileciteturn0file7

From the evidence reviewed, the headline result is straightforward. **Claude Desktop is the strongest fit for the stated office-worker use case**, especially when you combine **Cowork** for local/macOS tasks with **Claude for Microsoft 365** for Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and SharePoint-adjacent workflows, and deploy it with Anthropic’s regional controls. **Manus** is the most ambitious generalist “do the whole task for me” product, but it is materially weaker on transparent local-first boundaries and EU-governance evidence in the sources reviewed. **Codex** is more capable for office work than its branding suggests, but its centre of gravity remains a controlled task runner with strong approvals and local tooling rather than a first-rate office-suite-native assistant. citeturn6view0turn10view0turn7view1turn28view1turn27view3turn24view4turn25view1

## Architecture and data-boundary comparison

The most privacy-relevant distinction is between **Claude Chat** and **Claude Cowork**. Anthropic’s own MCP announcement says all Claude.ai plans support connecting MCP servers to the **Claude Desktop app**, and the uploaded architecture analysis shows the desktop app loading `claude.ai`, with Anthropic-side infrastructure handling chat sessions, while Cowork uses a **local Ubuntu VM** on the Mac with **VirtioFS host-folder sharing** and a dedicated local networking stack. In practice, that means **Chat** behaves like a polished hosted assistant, whereas **Cowork** is much closer to a supervised local automation worker. For a GDPR-sensitive employer, that difference is not academic. citeturn5view1 fileciteturn0file8 fileciteturn0file7

Anthropic has also built the product line around enterprise deployment options that matter in Europe. Its regional compliance page states that Claude supports **GDPR**, **regional processing within the EU/EEA**, a **DPA**, support for data subject rights and deletion, and regional data residency or inference options through **AWS Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex, and Microsoft Foundry**. Anthropic’s Microsoft 365 product page goes further, stating that Claude for Microsoft 365 can connect through the customer’s preferred cloud provider and “works within your existing compliance framework.” For an EU company that wants the files to live in M365 or company-controlled cloud tenancy rather than in a consumer chatbot account, that is the strongest governance story of the three products reviewed. citeturn7view0turn7view1turn10view0

Manus’s public position is different. Manus markets itself as a desktop and cloud-connected agent, and its Team page says it offers **SSO**, **audit logs**, **SOC 2 Type II**, **ISO 27001**, and that it does **not train models on Team/Enterprise data**. Its security page adds ISO 27701 and says customer data is deleted upon leaving. Those are real positives. But the retrieved vendor pages do **not** provide the same level of public detail on **EU/EEA data residency**, **regional inference options**, or the concrete local-vs-cloud execution boundary that Anthropic does. The sources reviewed clearly show Manus as a highly integrated service with Slack and “more” tools, but not as a transparently local-first, EU-regional office stack. citeturn28view1turn27view1turn27view3turn28view2

Codex sits between those two poles. OpenAI’s original Codex launch described it as a **cloud-based** agent where each task runs in its own isolated environment, and OpenAI’s enterprise privacy page says business data is **not used to train models by default**, that customers own their inputs and outputs, and that admins can control connected internal sources. At the same time, the current Codex app documentation describes **local environments**, **local project runs**, **computer use on macOS**, configurable permission profiles including `:read-only`, `:workspace`, and `:danger-full-access`, and background automations that can run directly in the local project. So Codex today is neither purely remote nor purely local; it is a controlled hybrid with explicit security controls, but still fundamentally an OpenAI product whose model provider processes the work. citeturn4view0turn5view0turn24view2turn24view4turn24view5turn38view0turn38view5

That leads to the procurement-grade conclusion. If your policy means **“the AI vendor must never receive the file contents”**, then **none of these three products is an automatic fit**. If your policy instead means **“keep working files on the Mac, in M365/SharePoint, or in company-controlled cloud regions, while using enterprise controls, approvals, and auditing”**, then **Claude** is clearly the best-aligned option, **OpenAI/Codex** is workable with tighter scoping and custom controls, and **Manus** is the least evidenced choice in the current materials. citeturn7view1turn10view0turn5view0turn21view7turn28view1 fileciteturn0file8

## Workflow usefulness for a regular office worker

### Documents, spreadsheets, and slides

Claude is the strongest product here by a wide margin. **Claude Cowork** explicitly markets office tasks such as **organising files, building spreadsheets, preparing reports, and analysing notes**, and its examples show it working from attached folders, templates, CSV and JSON sources, and producing polished deliverables. More importantly, **Claude for Microsoft 365** is not just “AI beside Office”; Anthropic says it works **inside Excel, PowerPoint, Word, and Outlook**, can build a model from a brief, draft in company templates, preserve tracked changes and formatting, and create native charts and diagrams. Anthropic also says nothing is sent or saved until the user approves in the host app. For the stated use case, this is the closest thing to a purpose-built office-worker platform. citeturn6view0turn10view0

Manus is also very strong for content production. Its download and Slack pages market **slide generation**, cross-app tasking, teamwork in Slack, and support for outputs such as **presentations, documents, spreadsheets, project plans, and meeting notes**. Manus’s product positioning is more “give me the finished deliverable” than “assist me inside Word or Excel”, which many office workers will like. The trade-off is control and governance: the workflow is slick, but the public evidence for how well it preserves enterprise document structures, authoring controls, and regulated storage boundaries is weaker than Anthropic’s Microsoft 365 integration story. citeturn27view3turn28view2turn28view1

Codex is better than its reputation suggests, but it is still the least natural office suite tool of the three. OpenAI’s current Codex app docs say the sidebar can preview **PDF files, spreadsheets, documents, and presentations**, and that users can specify the expected file type, sheet structure, charts, and slide sections. That proves Codex can generate and review non-code office artefacts. But nothing in the retrieved Codex product docs approaches Claude’s native Office integration. For a finance analyst, PMO lead, executive assistant, or operations manager who lives in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, Codex looks like a capable sidecar rather than a native office copilot. citeturn25view1turn24view4

### CSV analysis, web research, and interactive reports

Claude again takes the lead, but now the gap narrows. Cowork’s public examples show CSV and JSON attachments, scheduled metric pulls, report preparation, and interactive data views where the user can hover and toggle to inspect patterns. That is a very office-friendly fit for recurring reporting and lightweight BI-style workflows. Anthropic’s connector and MCP model also mean Claude can be given access to internal data systems with a uniform mechanism instead of one-off hacks. citeturn6view0turn5view1turn12view5

Manus is plausibly excellent at broad research and synthesis. Its Team plan prominently advertises **Wide Research**, **scheduled tasks**, **data visualization**, and examples such as quarterly budget planning and extracting data online. It is probably the most “research-assistant-meets-operator” of the three in user experience terms. But the evidence retrieved is more marketing-level than control-level: the materials checked show strong breadth, not equally strong proof of boundary control, regionality, or enterprise review workflows. That matters for any company turning web and internal data into executive-facing outputs. citeturn28view1turn27view3

Codex has real strengths for structured research work, but they are not office-native strengths. The OpenAI docs show an **in-app browser**, **browser use**, **computer use**, scheduled automations, and non-code artifact previews. That means Codex can gather information from websites, inspect rendered outputs, and produce documents or spreadsheets. The friction is that these capabilities are framed as controlled task execution, not as a polished office-research workflow. The in-app browser also explicitly **does not support authentication flows, signed-in pages, cookies, extensions, or an existing browser profile**, which makes it weaker for real enterprise portals unless you switch to broader computer use or another surface. citeturn23view6turn24view1turn24view2turn25view1turn24view5

### Organising local folders and working on local files

This is where **Claude Cowork** looks especially strong. Anthropic’s Cowork examples explicitly show pointing Claude at a Downloads folder, having it propose folder categories, naming conventions, and review flags, and only then proceeding after approval. Combined with the uploaded architecture notes showing **local VM execution** and **host-folder mounting**, Cowork is unusually well aligned with the “please help me clean up my Mac and prepare a result” use case. That is not a developer use case; it is pure knowledge work. citeturn6view0 fileciteturn0file8 fileciteturn0file7

Codex can also do local-machine work, but it is more explicit and more dangerous by design. Its computer-use documentation says that on macOS the user must grant **Screen Recording** and **Accessibility** so Codex can see the target app and **click, type, and navigate**. Its permissions model allows read-only, workspace-write, or full-access profiles, and OpenAI warns that full-access automation carries elevated risk. That makes Codex potentially powerful for local office automations, but it is better suited to carefully-administered expert users than to broad office deployment. citeturn24view5turn25view0turn38view0turn38view1turn38view5

Manus certainly aims at this territory, but the public product pages retrieved describe connected tools, Slack, scheduled tasks, and deliverables more than local folder operations. The vendor pages reviewed do not provide office-focused evidence on governed local-file reorganisation comparable to Claude Cowork’s examples. That does not prove Manus cannot do it; it means the evidence base is thinner for procurement and governance purposes. citeturn27view3turn28view1turn28view2

### Meeting recordings, transcription, and meeting memos

This is the weakest area across all three products. In the retrieved sources, **Claude for Microsoft 365** and **Codex** both clearly support **voice dictation** into the assistant, but that is **not the same thing as batch transcription of MP3 meeting recordings**. Claude says you can “use dictation to talk to Claude in a sidebar”; Codex says it will transcribe your spoken prompt when you hold the shortcut and speak. Those are convenience features for prompting, not a fully documented meeting-transcription workflow. citeturn10view0turn24view1

Manus’s Slack integration page is strongest on **meeting summaries** and turning discussion context into next steps, but the sources reviewed do not document a clear first-party **MP3 upload and transcript generation** path. In other words: all three can probably help **after** text exists; none of the retrieved product materials makes them look like the best native replacement for a dedicated meeting-transcription product. For a privacy-conscious EU firm, that may actually be beneficial: it points toward keeping speech-to-text on a separate approved stack and using one of these tools only for memo drafting and follow-up actions. citeturn28view2turn10view0turn24view1

## Enterprise integrations and MCP

For **Jira and Confluence via MCP**, Claude has the cleanest first-principles story. Anthropic states that **all Claude.ai plans support connecting MCP servers to the Claude Desktop app**, and its connector directory is explicitly built on **MCP**. That means a company-hosted Atlassian MCP server is a natural fit for Claude Desktop in a way that does not depend on vendor-specific plugins. I did not retrieve a first-party Anthropic page naming Jira or Confluence specifically in Claude’s connector catalogue, so that part should be treated as an informed deployment inference rather than a proven checkbox. citeturn5view1turn12view5turn7view9

OpenAI’s story is more fragmented but still substantial. The broader ChatGPT platform supports **apps**, including **custom MCP-based apps**, and Business or Enterprise/Edu admins can restrict app availability, actions, RBAC, and sync behaviour, with app calls logged for compliance. Atlassian’s December 2025 Rovo announcement specifically brought **ChatGPT access to Jira and Confluence through its MCP ecosystem**, with **OAuth, permission controls, audit logs, and allowlists**. The caveat is important: the strongest evidence here is for **ChatGPT workspace apps**, not for the **Codex desktop app** specifically. Codex’s own MCP documentation currently says MCP is supported in the **CLI and IDE extension**, not prominently in the desktop app UI. So the OpenAI platform absolutely has an Atlassian/MCP route, but the cleanest route is **ChatGPT workspace + apps**, not plainly “Codex app on Mac”. citeturn21view6turn21view7turn36news0turn24view3

For **Slack**, all three have something real, but in different forms. Anthropic now has **Claude Tag in Slack**, which Reuters reported as a research preview for Team and Enterprise customers, with admin control over what data it can access. Manus has a clear Slack integration that reads thread context and generates deliverables like proposals, specs, meeting summaries, and spreadsheets. OpenAI’s Codex also has a Slack integration, but its docs frame it around creating **Codex cloud tasks** from channels and threads and require a connected GitHub environment, which makes it much less relevant for ordinary office staff than for technical teams. On office-work grounds, Slack supports **Claude and Manus better than Codex**. citeturn18news0turn28view2turn25view4

For **Microsoft 365 and SharePoint**, Claude is the clear winner. Anthropic’s Microsoft 365 product works inside **Excel, PowerPoint, Word, and Outlook** and can connect through the customer’s chosen cloud deployment model. Reporting on Anthropic’s Microsoft 365 connector also states it can work across **OneDrive and SharePoint**, which is exactly the storage pattern the user asked for. I did not retrieve equivalent first-party SharePoint evidence for Manus or Codex in this research pass. citeturn10view0turn34news0

## GDPR, security, and fit ranking

For a GDPR-constrained EU company, the ranking is not just about features. It is about how convincingly each vendor supports **regional processing**, **admin controls**, **auditability**, and **least-privilege operation**.

**Claude Desktop ranks first** because its product family spans the whole office workflow. **Chat** is the least privacy-favourable Claude mode because it is fundamentally hosted. **Cowork** is the most privacy-favourable because it runs a local VM on the Mac and is explicitly designed for file organisation, spreadsheets, reports, and note analysis. **Claude for Microsoft 365** then closes the last mile by meeting workers inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and the Microsoft storage ecosystem. Anthropic also has the strongest directly retrieved evidence on **GDPR**, **EU/EEA regional processing**, **DPA**, and multi-cloud deployment choices. citeturn6view0turn10view0turn7view0turn7view1 fileciteturn0file8 fileciteturn0file7

**Codex ranks second**. It has a more mature governance surface than many people assume: business-data no-training commitments, admin-managed apps, compliance logging, explicit permission profiles, approvals, local project modes, and macOS computer use with OS-level permission prompts. It can absolutely be used for some office-worker automations and structured report generation. But it is still not the most natural tool for Word/Excel/PowerPoint or SharePoint-centric work, and the strongest MCP and app-governance evidence sits in **ChatGPT workspaces**, not squarely in the Codex desktop app itself. citeturn5view0turn21view6turn21view7turn24view2turn24view5turn25view1turn38view0turn38view5

**Manus ranks third**. It may be the most exciting to watch for broad office automation: its public pages are full of studies, slides, research, scheduling, and cross-tool outcomes, and its Slack integration is compelling. It also has meaningful enterprise features including SSO, audit logs, and non-training commitments for Team or Enterprise users. But in the sources reviewed here, Manus has the weakest transparent evidence for **EU-regional data handling**, the weakest documentary evidence for **MCP to Atlassian**, and the weakest “work inside the approved enterprise file estate” story compared with Claude’s Microsoft 365 strategy. For a consumer or startup user, that may be acceptable. For a GDPR-sensitive company, it is a real disadvantage. citeturn28view1turn27view1turn27view3turn28view2

## Recommendation and limitations

For the exact brief given, the best deployment pattern is **Claude Desktop, but not as a single mode**. Use **Claude Cowork** for local directory work, recurring report assembly, and controlled file operations on macOS; use **Claude for Microsoft 365** for document, spreadsheet, deck, mailbox, and SharePoint-adjacent work; and use **MCP** to connect approved internal systems such as Jira and Confluence from company-controlled servers. That gives the best mix of worker usability and governance. citeturn6view0turn10view0turn5view1turn7view1

If Claude is not an option, the second-best path is **OpenAI on a tightly managed business or enterprise footing**, but the strongest integration story for Jira, Confluence, and other governed connectors is through **ChatGPT workspace apps and MCP**, not through the Codex desktop app alone. Codex itself is better viewed as a controlled local operator with office-capable side effects than as a native office-worker shell. citeturn21view6turn21view7turn24view3turn25view1turn24view5

The main limitations of this research are also worth stating clearly. I did **not** retrieve a vendor-published Manus document proving EU/EEA regional data residency or Atlassian MCP support; I did **not** retrieve first-party Codex desktop documentation positioning the desktop app itself as the primary MCP client for business tools; and I did **not** find strong first-party documentation from any of the three products for **native MP3 batch transcription** as a core office workflow.
