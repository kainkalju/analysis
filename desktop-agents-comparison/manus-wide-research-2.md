# Canon EOS R5 vs. Canon EOS R5 Mark II — Comprehensive Comparison

> **Source:** [The Digital Picture — Canon EOS R5 Review](https://www.the-digital-picture.com/Reviews/Canon-EOS-R5.aspx) and [Canon EOS R5 Mark II Review](https://www.the-digital-picture.com/Reviews/Canon-EOS-R5-Mark-II.aspx)

## Overview

The **Canon EOS R5** and the **Canon EOS R5 Mark II** represent two generations of Canon's flagship full-frame mirrorless camera. The original R5 launched as a landmark product, offering 45 megapixels, 8K RAW video, and a best-in-class autofocus system. The Mark II refines and elevates nearly every aspect of its predecessor — from sensor architecture and burst speed to video capabilities and autofocus intelligence — while addressing the most significant criticism of the original: overheating.

## Sensor Architecture

The most fundamental hardware change between the two models is the shift from a standard **Dual Pixel CMOS** sensor in the R5 to a **Backside-Illuminated (BSI) CMOS** sensor in the R5 Mark II. BSI architecture repositions the wiring layer behind the photodiodes, allowing more light to reach each pixel. This translates to improved low-light performance, better dynamic range, and — critically — enables the dramatically faster **6.3 ms sensor readout** that underpins the 30 fps electronic burst and the greatly reduced rolling shutter distortion. Both sensors share the same 45 MP resolution and physical dimensions.

---

## Autofocus

Both cameras feature Canon's Dual Pixel CMOS AF technology, but the Mark II represents a substantial generational leap. The original R5 offers **1,053 AF areas** with 5,940 manually selectable points, covering approximately 100% of the frame, and supports deep-learning-based subject tracking for people and animals down to **EV −6.0**.

The R5 Mark II introduces **Eye Control AF**, which allows the photographer to direct the initial AF tracking point simply by looking at the subject through the viewfinder — a feature unique to Canon's lineup. It also extends the low-light AF threshold to **EV −7.5**, nearly a full stop darker than its predecessor, and adds **Action Priority AF** for improved tracking of fast-moving subjects. The combination of faster readout, improved algorithms, and Eye Control AF makes the Mark II's autofocus system arguably the most capable available in any mirrorless camera.

---

## Video Capabilities

Video is the area where the Mark II most dramatically surpasses the original. The R5's video capabilities were impressive on paper — 8K RAW, 4K 120p, 10-bit 4:2:2 — but were severely constrained by **thermal shutdowns** that limited 8K recording to approximately 20 minutes and 4K 60p to approximately 25 minutes at room temperature.

The R5 Mark II resolves this with a redesigned thermal management system, enabling **up to 6 hours of continuous recording** (excluding high frame rate modes). It also raises the ceiling significantly: **8K DCI at 60p RAW** (double the original's 30p cap), **2K DCI at 240p** for ultra-slow-motion, simultaneous **proxy recording**, **3–5 second pre-recording**, **Canon Log 2 and Log 3** color profiles, **LPCM 24-bit 4-channel audio**, and a **full-size HDMI Type-A** port replacing the awkward micro HDMI of the original. For video professionals, the Mark II is an entirely different proposition.

## Speed & Buffer

| Metric | EOS R5 | EOS R5 Mark II |
|---|---|---|
| Electronic burst | 20 fps | **30 fps** (full 14-bit RAW) |
| Mechanical burst | 12 fps | 12 fps |
| Pre-capture | No | **Yes** (3 or 5 s) |
| Rolling shutter | Noticeable | Greatly reduced (6.3 ms readout) |

The 30 fps electronic burst at full 14-bit lossless compressed RAW with continuous AF and AE is a landmark achievement. Combined with pre-continuous shooting — which captures frames before the shutter button is fully pressed — the Mark II ensures that decisive moments are never missed. The 6.3 ms readout also makes the electronic shutter far more practical for sports and action photography than on the original R5.

---

## In-Body Image Stabilization (IBIS)

Both cameras include IBIS, with the R5 rated at **up to 8 stops** of correction and the R5 Mark II improving this to **up to 8.5 stops**. While the half-stop improvement is modest, the Mark II also adds **Movie Digital IS coordinated control**, which combines IBIS with optical and digital stabilization for smoother handheld video.

---

## Connectivity & Build

The Mark II upgrades connectivity across the board: **Wi-Fi 6E** (replacing standard Wi-Fi), **Bluetooth 5.0**, a **full-size HDMI Type-A** port, and **USB 3.2 Gen 2**. The new **multi-function accessory shoe** supports both data communication and power delivery, enabling a wider ecosystem of accessories. Both cameras share nearly identical dimensions and the same 738 g body weight, with the same dual card slot configuration (CFexpress Type B + UHS-II SD) and weather-sealed magnesium alloy construction.

---

## In-Camera Processing Intelligence

A notable addition in the Mark II is **neural network-based in-camera processing**, including:

- **2× upscaling** of images to approximately 179 megapixels
- **In-camera noise reduction** powered by AI

These features extend the creative and practical utility of the camera without requiring a computer, representing a meaningful step toward computational photography in a professional body.

## Pros and Cons Summary

### Canon EOS R5

**Strengths:** Exceptional 45 MP image quality; class-leading (at launch) autofocus with deep learning subject tracking; 8K RAW and 4K 120p video; IBIS; dual card slots; robust weather sealing; excellent ergonomics.

**Weaknesses:** Significant video overheating limits recording duration; micro HDMI port is fragile and inconvenient; electronic shutter produces noticeable rolling shutter; high price; large RAW files demand fast storage and powerful computers.

### Canon EOS R5 Mark II

**Strengths:** BSI sensor with 6.3 ms readout; 30 fps full 14-bit RAW burst; Eye Control AF; AF at EV −7.5; 8K 60p RAW video with no meaningful recording limit; pre-continuous shooting; Wi-Fi 6E; full-size HDMI; in-camera neural network upscaling and noise reduction; 8.5-stop IBIS.

**Weaknesses:** IBIS produces an audible hum in quiet environments; minor UI annoyances (mode-switching message, temperature warning on startup); pre-continuous shooting cannot be toggled via a custom button; moderately higher price than the original R5.

## Conclusion

The Canon EOS R5 Mark II is an unambiguous generational improvement over the original R5 in virtually every measurable category. The BSI sensor architecture, 30 fps full-RAW electronic burst, resolved overheating, Eye Control AF, full-size HDMI, Wi-Fi 6E, and in-camera neural network processing collectively represent a camera that is not merely an incremental update but a substantially more capable tool. The $100 price premium over the original R5's launch price is negligible given the scope of improvements.

For photographers and videographers who already own the original R5, the upgrade is compelling primarily for those who shoot video seriously, require the highest burst rates, or work in very low light. For those entering the system new, the R5 Mark II is the clear recommendation at its price point.
