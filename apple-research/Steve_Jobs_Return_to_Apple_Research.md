# Steve Jobs Returning to Apple: Key Points & Timeline

## Executive Summary
Steve Jobs left Apple in 1985 after a conflict with CEO John Sculley, founded NeXT, and later returned to Apple in 1997 when the company acquired NeXT. His return marked one of the greatest corporate comebacks, transforming Apple from near-bankruptcy to becoming one of the world's most valuable companies.

---

## Pre-Return Timeline: Jobs' Departure & Years Away

### **September 17, 1985 - Jobs Leaves Apple**
- Jobs and Sculley had a major power struggle over the company's direction
- Jobs wanted to focus on the closed-architecture Macintosh
- Sculley favored open-architecture computers like the Apple II
- Jobs formally submitted his resignation letter to the Apple Board
- Five additional senior Apple employees resigned and joined Jobs

### **1985 - NeXT Founded**
- Jobs founded NeXT Inc. with $7 million initial funding
- The company specialized in computer platforms for higher-education and business markets
- Jobs served as CEO

### **1986 - Pixar Investment**
- Jobs purchased the computer graphics division of Lucasfilm
- The division was spun off as Pixar Animation Studios
- Jobs became its chairman and majority shareholder

### **October 12, 1988 - NeXT Computer Unveiled**
- Lavish launch event at Louise M. Davies Symphony Hall, San Francisco
- Described as Jobs's "comeback event"
- Positioned as a multimedia extravaganza

### **1990 - NeXT Workstations Released**
- Priced at $9,999 (equivalent to ~$25,000 in 2025)
- Known for technical strengths including object-oriented software development
- Made use of Tim Berners-Lee's World Wide Web invention (created on NeXT computers at CERN)

### **1993 - NeXT Transitions to Software**
- Hardware division ended after selling only 50,000 machines
- Company shifted focus to software development with release of NeXTSTEP/Intel

### **1994 - NeXT Reports First Yearly Profit**
- Company earned $1.03 million in annual profit

### **1996 - WebObjects Released**
- NeXT Software released WebObjects framework for Web application development
- Later used to build Apple Store, MobileMe services, and iTunes Store

---

## The Return: 1996-1997

### **December 1996 - Apple Acquires NeXT**
- Apple negotiated with Be Inc. but ultimately chose NeXT
- Purchase price: $400 million
- Deal finalized in February 1997
- Brought Jobs back to the company he co-founded

### **February 1997 - Deal Officially Finalized**
- Much of NeXT's technology incorporated into Apple products
- NeXTSTEP evolved into Mac OS X

### **July 1997 - Jobs Becomes De Facto Chief**
- CEO Gil Amelio was ousted
- Jobs assumed de facto leadership role

### **September 16, 1997 - Interim CEO Appointment**
- Jobs formally named interim chief executive officer

---

## Early Years of Jobs' Return (1997-2000)

### **March 1998 - Project Terminations**
- Jobs terminated several projects to focus Apple on profitability:
  - Newton (personal digital assistant)
  - Cyberdog (web browser)
  - OpenDoc (component software system)
- Created significant uncertainty among employees; elevator rides became feared

### **1998 - iMac Introduction**
- Designed by Jonathan Ive (designer who worked closely with Jobs)
- Stylish, colorful design that revitalized Apple's image
- Introduced as part of a return-to-profitability strategy

### **January 9, 2001 - iTunes Announced**
- Steve Jobs announced iTunes to the public
- Initially designed as music library organization and storage for Mac users
- Promoted as "World's Best and Easiest To Use Jukebox Software"

### **October 16, 2003 - iTunes for Windows Released**
- Extended reach of iTunes beyond Mac users
- Expanded market potential significantly

### **April 28, 2003 - iTunes Music Store Opened**
- 200,000 tracks available at launch
- Over 1 million songs purchased in first week
- Music protected by FairPlay encryption (DRM)

### **2000 Macworld Expo - CEO Title Confirmed**
- Jobs officially dropped the "interim" modifier from his CEO title
- Became permanent CEO of Apple
- Jobs joked he would use the title "iCEO"

---

## Major Product Launches During Jobs' Leadership (1997-2011)

### **iPod Era**
- **Introduction of iPod portable music player** - Integrated with iTunes
- Became transformational product that established Apple in consumer electronics
- Combined with iTunes ecosystem for unprecedented user experience

### **iPad Launch**
- **January 27, 2010** - iPad first announced
- **April 3, 2010** - Wi-Fi version released in US
- **April 30, 2010** - 3G-capable version released
- Innovation that created new product category: tablet computers

### **iPhone**
- **June 29, 2007** - Apple entered cellular phone business
- Multi-touch display phone that revolutionized mobile browsing
- Integrated iPod features with mobile capabilities

### **Related Services**
- **iTunes Store** - Digital music distribution platform
- **App Store** - July 10, 2008 - Mobile app marketplace for iOS
- **Mac OS X** - Operating system evolved from NeXT technology

---

## Design Partnership: Steve Jobs & Jony Ive

### **Key Collaboration**
- Jobs worked closely with British designer Jonathan Ive starting in 1997
- Ive served as Chief Design Officer
- Together they created iconic products:
  - iMac (various generations including original lamp-style design)
  - iPod
  - iPhone
  - iPad
- Jobs and Ive are named as co-inventors in approximately 200 patents together

### **Design Philosophy**
- Jobs emphasized simplicity and elegance
- Influenced by Zen Buddhism and minimalism
- Focus on "the look and feel of the product"
- Integration of hardware and software design

## Disney/Pixar Integration

### **2003-2004 - Pixar Contract Negotiations**
- Jobs and Disney CEO Michael Eisner failed to negotiate new partnership
- January 2004 - Jobs announced he would never deal with Disney again

### **October 2005 - Bob Iger Takes Over Disney**
- New Disney CEO quickly worked to mend Pixar relationship with Jobs

### **January 24, 2006 - Disney Acquires Pixar**
- All-stock transaction worth $7.4 billion
- Jobs became Disney's largest single shareholder (~7% of stock)
- Jobs joined Disney's board of directors
- Significantly exceeded holdings of Michael Eisner (1.7%)

### **Pixar's Success Under Jobs' Leadership**
Notable films produced during Jobs' life:
- Toy Story (1995)
- A Bug's Life (1998)
- Toy Story 2 (1999)
- Monsters, Inc. (2001)
- Finding Nemo (2003)
- The Incredibles (2004)
- Cars (2006)
- Ratatouille (2007)
- WALL-E (2008)
- Up (2009)
- Toy Story 3 (2010)
- Cars 2 (2011)

Multiple Academy Awards for Best Animated Feature won by Pixar films

## Apple's Transformation During Jobs' Second Era

### **Key Achievements**
1. **Saved Apple from near-bankruptcy** (1997) to becoming world's most valuable company (2011)
2. **Product diversification** - Shifted from computers to consumer electronics
3. **Market leadership** - Dominated multiple product categories
4. **Brand transformation** - From struggling company to design and innovation leader
5. **Revenue streams** - iTunes ecosystem created new business model

### **Financial Success**
- Apple's market capitalization exceeded Dell's in 2006
- Jobs sent celebratory email to employees: "it turned out that Michael Dell wasn't perfect at predicting the future"
- Company propelled to become world's most valuable publicly traded company (2011)

## Health Challenges & Leadership Continuity

### **October 2003 - Cancer Diagnosis**
- Jobs diagnosed with pancreatic cancer

### **Mid 2004 - Announcement to Employees**
- Announced pancreatic tumor diagnosis
- Had rare, less aggressive type: islet cell neuroendocrine tumor

### **July 2004 - Surgery**
- Underwent pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple procedure)
- Appeared to successfully remove tumor
- Resisted chemotherapy and radiation

### **January 2006 - Cancer Recurrence**
- Only Jobs' wife, doctors, and Disney CEO Bob Iger knew cancer had returned

### **2006 & 2008 - Public Speculation**
- Thin, gaunt appearance at keynotes sparked health concerns
- Apple officials denied serious health issues
- August 28, 2008 - Bloomberg accidentally published Jobs' obituary

### **August 2008 - Health Update**
- Jobs responded at Let's Rock keynote, paraphrasing Mark Twain
- Presented slide with blood pressure reading "110/70"

### **January 5, 2009 - Hormone Imbalance Announcement**
- Jobs stated he'd been suffering from hormone imbalance

### **January 14, 2009 - Health-Related Absence**
- Jobs announced six-month leave of absence
- Tim Cook became acting CEO
- Jobs remained involved in "major strategic decisions"

### **April 2009 - Liver Transplant**
- Jobs underwent liver transplantation at Methodist University Hospital, Memphis, Tennessee
- Prognosis described as "excellent"

### **January 17, 2011 - Second Medical Leave**
- Another leave of absence announced
- Appeared at iPad 2 launch (March 2), WWDC keynote (June 6), and Cupertino City Council (June 7)

### **August 24, 2011 - Resignation as CEO**
- Jobs announced his resignation
- Wrote to board: "If there ever came a day when I could no longer meet my duties and expectations as Apple's CEO, I would be the first to let you know. Unfortunately, that day has come."
- Tim Cook named as successor
- Jobs became chairman of the board
- Continued working until day before his death

### **October 5, 2011 - Steve Jobs Dies**
- Died at his home in Palo Alto, California
- Age 56
- Due to complications from pancreatic cancer relapse
- Surrounded by family and sisters at time of death
- Final words: "Oh wow. Oh wow. Oh wow."
- Private funeral October 7, 2011

## Patents & Design Recognition

### **Patent Portfolio**
- Listed as primary inventor or co-inventor in 346 US patents/patent applications
- 200 patents co-credited with Jonathan Ive
- 43 issued US patents on inventions
- Majority are design patents focused on "look and feel"
- Patent on Mac OS X Dock magnification feature issued day before his death

### **Awards**
- Presidential Medal of Freedom (awarded posthumously 2022)
- Recognition as pioneer of personal computer revolution

## Key Metrics: Before vs. After Return

### **1997 (Upon Return)**
- Apple near bankruptcy
- Market share declining
- Multiple failed products (Newton, etc.)
- Uncertain future

### **2011 (Upon Resignation)**
- World's most valuable publicly traded company
- Market leader in multiple product categories:
  - Computers
  - Tablets
  - Smartphones
  - Digital media
- 677.7 million iPads sold (by 2022)
- Transformed entire industries

---

## Conclusion

Steve Jobs' return to Apple in 1997 represents one of the greatest business turnarounds in history. Through strategic vision, partnership with designer Jonathan Ive, acquisition of NeXT technology, and relentless focus on user experience, Jobs transformed Apple from a struggling computer maker into a global technology and consumer electronics powerhouse that fundamentally changed multiple industries including computing, music, mobile phones, and tablets.

His 14-year leadership from return to resignation (1997-2011) resulted in products that shaped the modern digital age and established design and innovation standards that continue to influence technology companies today.
